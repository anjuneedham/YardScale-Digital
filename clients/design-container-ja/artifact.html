Paying for it

Three routes people
actually use.

We are not a lender and we will not pretend financing is simple. But we know what the institutions ask for, and we prepare it before you are asked.

01

Credit union facility

Several Jamaican credit unions lend against prefabricated housing. They will want clear registered title to the land and a stamped engineer's report — both of which we supply as part of the contract.

02

Staged self-funding

Start with a studio you can pay for outright, live in it, and add a module later. The structural design allows for it from day one, so nothing is wasted.

03

Rental-backed

A well-sited unit lets short-let income service the borrowing. We will model it with you honestly, including the months when occupancy is poor.

On importing it yourself. People ask. Prefabricated buildings land under HS 9406 at 20% duty on the CIF value, then GCT on the duty-inclusive figure, plus stamp duty — roughly a third on top before it leaves the wharf, and site works are still ahead of you. The guide works a full example through. Confirm the current rates with a licensed customs broker before you commit.

Straight answers

The things people ask
before they trust us.

Will it hold up in a hurricane?

The steel shell is the easy part — a container is built to be stacked nine high and thrown around the Atlantic. What matters is the connection to the ground. We anchor to a cast reinforced foundation through engineered hold-down plates at every corner casting, designed to CUBiC wind loading for a 150 mph gust and signed off by a registered structural engineer. That drawing set goes to parish council and to your insurer.

Does it get unbearably hot inside?

An uninsulated container does, yes. Ours are sprayed internally with 50 mm of closed-cell foam, which both insulates and stops condensation forming on the inside face of the steel. Every model also carries a ventilated roof canopy that keeps direct sun off the container entirely — that canopy does more for comfort than any air conditioner.

Can I get a mortgage on it?

Conventional mortgages remain difficult for prefabricated housing in Jamaica. In practice most of our clients use a credit union facility, staged self-funding, or rental income. What every one of them needs is clear registered title and a stamped engineer's report, and we prepare both. The buyer's guide names the institutions currently lending and what each asks for.

Do I need planning approval?

For permanent siting, yes — building approval from your parish council, or NEPA for larger developments. The submission needs site plans, structural drawings and evidence of compliance with the Building Act 2018. We prepare and lodge that package and track it through to approval. Budget six to twelve weeks, and note that it runs in parallel with fabrication, not after it.

What does the price actually include?

The unit, complete — kitchen, bathroom, electrical, plumbing, insulation, flooring, joinery, doors and glazing — delivered and craned onto your prepared foundation within 40 km of Kingston. Separately quoted, once we have seen your site: land, foundation, septic or sewer connection, water and NWC or JPS connections, parish council fees, and delivery beyond 40 km. We give you those numbers before you sign, not after.

How long will it last?

The structural steel carries a 25-year warranty. Realistically, a properly coated and maintained unit outlives that comfortably — the limiting factor is the coating system, and recoating every ten to twelve years is a weekend's work, not a rebuild.

Can I see one before I commit?

Yes, and you should. Never buy a container home you have not stood inside. Book a viewing and we will walk you through a completed unit — bring anyone whose opinion you trust.
