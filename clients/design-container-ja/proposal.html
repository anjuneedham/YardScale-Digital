Where you are now

The problem is not
demand. It is proof.

People are already interested in container homes in Jamaica. What they cannot easily do is find you, understand what you charge, and convince themselves you are legitimate — all before they are willing to call.

Right now the business lives on social media. That works well for reach and terribly for conversion, for three reasons:

Social sells the look. It cannot sell the decision.

A buyer scrolling past a photograph is impressed. A buyer about to spend eight million dollars needs specifications, a price range, a process, and evidence that the structure is engineered and approvable. None of that fits in a caption, so the conversation stalls at "DM for price" — and most people never do.

Every enquiry arrives cold, and lands in one inbox.

DMs and WhatsApp messages have no structure. You are answering the same six questions by hand, several times a week, to people who may not own land yet. That is expensive in the only resource you cannot buy more of.

You do not own the audience.

A follower is rented. If the platform changes what it shows, your pipeline changes with it, and there is nothing you can do. An email list of people who have raised their hand is an asset you keep.

The gap in one sentence

There is no place a serious buyer can go to answer their own questions at eleven at night — which is exactly when people make decisions about houses.

A premium single-page site that answers every question a buyer has before they contact you — models, prices, process, hurricane engineering, financing routes and approvals — so the people who do call are already most of the way to yes.

02

The buyer's guide

A 20-page PDF worth having: costs, import duty, parish council approvals, financing, and a site-readiness checklist. Given away free in exchange for a name and an email.

03

The lead system

The guide sends itself. Every enquiry is captured with the parish, the model of interest and the site notes already attached, and reaches you by email and WhatsApp within seconds.

Why the guide is the important part

Most people who encounter your business are not ready to buy today. They are eight months out, still deciding whether to build at all. A brochure site loses every one of them; there is nothing to do but leave.

The guide gives them a reason to hand over their details while they are still researching — and gives you permission to stay in touch until they are ready. It also does something a sales page cannot: it makes you the person who told them the truth about duty, approvals and what the price does not cover. That is the position you want to hold when they finally choose a builder.
